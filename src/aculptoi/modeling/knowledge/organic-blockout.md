# Organic blockout

## Construction guidance

Start with one dominant mass that establishes the subject's volume and silhouette. Use
primitives as temporary blockout geometry when useful, but combine overlapping masses
before treating an organic body as complete. Shape large proportions before adding
small features.

## Evaluation signals

The main body should read as one intentional volume from more than one view. Look for a
clear primary mass, coherent silhouette, and major features that appear integrated rather
than merely placed nearby.

## Common failure modes

Visible seams between primitive masses, a chain of repeated blobs, small details added
before the main body reads, and appendages that conceal an unresolved primary silhouette.
